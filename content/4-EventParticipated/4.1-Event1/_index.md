# “Cloud Architect” Event Report

### Event Information

- **Event name:** Cloud Architect
- **Date:** June 20, 2026
- **Role:** Attendee
- **Scale:** 8 teams with 5 members per team

### Event Objectives

- Provide a practical academic competition focused on Cloud Computing and system architecture design.
- Develop quick thinking and teamwork under time pressure.
- Reinforce knowledge of AWS services and system architecture through competition.
- Create opportunities for interns to meet and connect.

### Competition Rules

- Two teams competed directly and answered questions of increasing difficulty in turn.
- The team with the higher score advanced to the next round.
- If both teams had the same score, a tiebreaker question was used and the faster team won.
- The competition included 8 teams, each consisting of 5 members.

#### Special Abilities

- **Minimum Risk** *(once)*: A team could use this ability when uncertain about an answer. An incorrect answer caused no point deduction, while a correct answer earned half of the question's points.
- **Hope Star** *(once)*: A team could use this ability when highly confident. A correct answer doubled the points, but an incorrect answer also doubled the deduction.

### Question Topics

#### Core AWS Services

- **Amazon EC2 (Elastic Compute Cloud):** Provides virtual servers in the cloud with control over the operating system and infrastructure configuration.
- **Amazon S3 (Simple Storage Service):** An object storage service in the cloud.
- **Amazon RDS:** An AWS-managed relational database service.
- **AWS Lambda:** A serverless compute service that runs code in response to events without requiring direct server management.
- **Amazon ECS:** A service for orchestrating and running containers on AWS.
- **AWS Elastic Beanstalk:** Supports application deployment while AWS manages most of the related infrastructure.

#### System Architecture

- **Microservices Architecture:** Divides a system into independent services, each responsible for a specific function.
- **Event-Driven Architecture:** Covers Publish/Subscribe, Point-to-point, and Streaming patterns.
- **Domain-Driven Design (DDD):** Designs systems around business domains and business rules.
- **Serverless, Containers, and VMs:** Examines how to select an appropriate compute model for each use case.

#### Cloud Strategy

- **Scaling:** Differentiates scale up—adding resources to a machine—from scale out—adding more machines.
- **Cost Optimization:** Involves monitoring and removing unused resources to prevent unnecessary expenses.
- **Shared Responsibility Model:** Explains how management responsibilities change across EC2, ECS, Fargate, and Lambda.

### Participation Activities

- I watched the teams compete, listened to each question, and considered my own answer before the organizers announced the result.
- By observing the teams' discussions and strategies, I learned how AWS knowledge, quick responses, and teamwork were applied under time pressure.

### What I Learned

#### Cloud and AWS Knowledge

- I gained a clearer understanding of the differences and use cases among Amazon EC2, Amazon ECS, AWS Elastic Beanstalk, and AWS Lambda.
- I learned when serverless may be more appropriate than traditional servers.
- I understood how AWS costs can arise and why unused resources such as snapshots or idle instances should be removed.
- I reinforced my understanding of synchronous and asynchronous communication, publish/subscribe, streaming, and the trade-offs of each pattern.

#### Teamwork Skills

- By observing the competition, I learned how teams assigned questions according to each member's strengths.
- I saw that concise communication and quick agreement had a major effect on results.
- I learned how teams managed risk by choosing when to use special abilities instead of answering without sufficient confidence.

#### Quick-Thinking Skills

- Following time-limited questions helped me practice faster thinking than studying theory alone.
- I learned the value of recognizing knowledge gaps and choosing a lower-risk approach when uncertain.
- I actively analyzed questions and eliminated incorrect options before hearing the official answers.

#### Networking

- I had opportunities to meet and learn from interns in other teams.
- I expanded my connections within the cloud and technology community.
- I gained a clearer view of my peers' knowledge levels and used it to plan my own study priorities.

### Event Experience

Attending **Cloud Architect** was an enjoyable and valuable experience that allowed me to approach Cloud Computing through a lively competition. It was my first time watching a technical knowledge game show, where time pressure and competition created a very different learning environment from ordinary study.

While watching the teams, I considered each question and compared my answer with the announced result. This helped me identify knowledge gaps, particularly in more advanced AWS services. The “Minimum Risk” and “Hope Star” abilities also showed that teams needed to balance confidence, points, and risk rather than simply choose an answer.

The team competition demonstrated the importance of fast and effective communication. Teams sometimes had only a few seconds to discuss and agree on an answer. Because each member had strengths in a different area, assigning the right person and coordinating effectively were more valuable than relying on one individual's ability.

### Lessons Learned

- Theory should be combined with practice and regular review; reading documentation alone is not enough without using the services directly.
- Questions I could not answer helped me identify topics to study before participating in a real project.
- I need to review AWS services and system architecture more systematically and spend more time practicing in the AWS Management Console.

> Overall, Cloud Architect was more than a competition. It helped me clearly assess what I understood and what I still needed to improve, making it a practical and valuable learning experience.
